Experimental research tooling. No Pokémon assets are included here.

AnimaEngine was consulted for Nitro formats, decompression, composition and
timeline behavior. Its MIT license and pinned revision are in
ANIMAENGINE-LICENSE.txt.

The experimental native-file reader accepts the observed linear 4-bit NCGR
battle sheet with mapping mode 0 and the 16-color NCLR palette whose TTLP
fields at offsets 8 and 12 are 0x000a0004 and 0. These fields were observed in
all 1,506 normal/shiny palette members of the privately supplied French Black 2
ROM; the check is a boundary for this prototype, not a universal Nitro rule.
Other mapping modes are rejected until their interpretation is verified. With
that ROM, AnimaEngine exported five species; front/back and
normal/shiny produced 20 atlas/palette combinations matching its 3x PNG output
after normalizing transparent RGB. For 4,131 NANR frames across ten views,
frame fields and NMCR records matched its JSON after normalizing 112 labels
for identity transforms (format 0 versus 3); their transform values matched.
Oshawott's front atlas indices matched the previous private source and its
controller produced identical node states for 240 ticks. These are sample
checks, not proof of complete species coverage, original-game playback, pixel
rasterization or Player integration. The reader expects decompressed,
preselected files and does not verify their common species, side, ROM or
archive identity. No extracted ROM assets are included in the repository.

The native-view boundary accepts version 0x0100 for NCER, NANR, NMCR and
NMAR. This version was observed in each of 1,502 matching headers per type
among the front/back member positions examined in that ROM. Four other
positions per type did not contain the expected file signature and were not
counted as matching files. This is a scope limit for the prototype, not a
claim about every valid Nitro file.

The native-view boundary bounds possible NCER parts by summing, for each
shared animation slot, its record count times the largest visible cell in the
slot's reachable sequence. A sum above 65,536 is rejected before rendering.
This is a conservative bound: independent slots may never reach their maxima
at the same time, so it can reject a view that would remain within the runtime
limit. The rejection means safety is not established, not that an overflow
has been proved for every rejected view.

The NCLR reader expands each five-bit color channel by bit replication, as
AnimaEngine does. The previous private JASC palette uses a different expansion
and may differ by one per eight-bit channel; on the Oshawott normal palette,
23 of 48 channels differed by +1 and the other 25 matched. Equality of palette
indices therefore does not imply byte-identical expanded colors across those
two representations.

src/system/mcss.c:363-398, 499-510 and 1436-1448. Calculations in the

In a second private melonDS 1.1 capture, successive paused states had frame
counters 72184 and 72185. The earlier state's camera and Moustillon position
projected a nine-quad prototype pose within 0.403 pixel per axis of all 36
vertices in the later state's render bank. The capture regression retains only
numeric geometry and matrices, not game media or the private savestate. The
source atlas indices and normal palette matched the earlier state's VRAM in
that experiment. A prototype raster approximately matched a JPEG capture after
an empirically fitted screen translation; it is not a lossless pixel comparison
and does not validate blending, the full animation or Player integration.

The experimental anima_frame_clock C helper resolves a bounded, forward-looping
parent frame and its elapsed tick, skipping zero-duration frames. It rejects
an all-zero timeline, other playback modes and nonzero loop starts. In a private
FFI bench it was used with the MIT AnimaEngine composer to reset observed
mode-zero children on parent-frame
changes. Five retained Oshawott software-GPU captures from one battle favored
that candidate over the composer's global child tick after per-frame integer
translation; one comparison matched all 688 visible reference pixels. This
is not proof of general timing, animation fidelity or Player integration.

The experiment compared against the existing controller and captures described
above. Those earlier investigations consulted GPL-licensed pokebw2 and
melonDS sources; their provenance and unresolved licensing questions continue
to apply. No melonDS raster source is included in the clock helper. No game
assets are included in this helper or its synthetic test.

The companion child-clock helper uses the elapsed parent-frame time for mode 0
and the supplied completed parent time plus elapsed time for mode 1. Each mode-1
input is bounded by 65535; their sum is not truncated. The caller must provide
the actual completed time, including any wrapping at a parent-frame transition;
a tick within the current parent loop is insufficient. Other child modes are
rejected.

The mode-1 and zero-duration rules were checked using a private software-emulator
experiment in which a copied battle state was modified in memory. Four unmodified
GPU frames reproduced earlier captures byte-for-byte. Across eight capture times
per variant, the clock agreed with 24 parent and 183 child states; ten GPU-image
comparisons remained imperfect except for two exact visible-pixel matches after
separate integer-translation fits. Each GPU comparison used a CPU snapshot two
captured steps earlier in this pipeline; all ten parent and 75 child-cell
selections matched. The mode-1 flags and zero duration were deliberately
altered, so this is not proof for naturally occurring mode-1 assets, mixed modes,
counter rollover, full rendering fidelity or physical hardware.

An unmodified Ponchiot back view from Bianca's tutorial naturally uses mode 1
and includes zero-duration parent frames. In a second controlled experiment,
only the completed parent counter in a private copy of that battle state was
changed. At the next parent-frame transition, it became 65534. While that
frame remained active, the 11 child states matched untruncated child times
65534 through 65539; at times 65536 through 65539, all 44 observations disagreed
with truncating the sum to 16 bits. At the following parent-frame transition,
the observed completed counter became 4, and the 11 children matched time 4.
Across ten snapshots, 110 child states matched the updated C helper, and 110
cell selections matched through the private AnimaEngine FFI bench. This is a
controlled counter mutation on one species, not a naturally elapsed 65536-tick
run or proof for every animation. No ROM assets or emulator raster source are
included in the helper or its tests.

In the same capture pipeline, two captured emulator steps are not a universal
CPU-to-GPU synchronization offset. At two transitions, matching texture regions
alone did not reveal mismatched angles in the prior CPU snapshot. These results
do not establish a general frame pairing or pixel-perfect composition.
